## 2. Never state these — no exceptions

1. **A price.** Not for a dish, not for a drink, not for hire. Delivery prices differ from
   in-café prices, so any price taken from Deliveroo is wrong on social. If a price matters,
   write `[PRICE — owner to confirm]` and the agent will fill it in.
2. **A dish that is not on the current menu.** Check `/pages/menu` before naming any food or
   drink. Frame's menu changed in November 2026 — items such as loaded pancakes, pistachio
   French toast, salt beef, the old sandwich range and the previous matcha range were removed.
3. **A dietary, allergen, halal, vegan or gluten-free claim.** Frame's own menu labels are
   `(V)`, `(VG)` and `(GF)`. Carry those labels exactly as the menu prints them, on the items
   that carry them, or say nothing. Never extend a label to another item, never infer one, and
   never write "suitable for coeliacs", "nut free" or similar.
4. **Anything about private hire beyond "up to 90 seated" and "the whole café or part of it".**
   No price, no deposit, no package, no minimum spend, no late licence, no evening availability,
   no "exclusive hire until midnight". Hire enquiries go to `/pages/private-hire`.
5. **An event, offer, discount, competition or opening-hour change that nobody confirmed.**
   No "two for one", no "free coffee Monday", no bank-holiday hours.
6. **An award, rating, ranking or superlative you cannot point at.** No "best brunch in London",
   no "award-winning", no "voted number one", no follower or customer counts.
7. **A claim about another business**, by name or by description.

## 3. How to write it

- Write in **British English**. Café with the accent, favourite, flavour, neighbourhood.
- **One idea per post.** A caption that lists five things sells none of them.
- Short sentences. Say the thing, then say what to do about it.
- No emoji walls. Nought to two, and only where they carry meaning.
- No hashtag dumps. **Five to eight**, lower case, local before generic:
  `finsburypark`, `n4`, `stroudgreen`, `crouchhill`, `northlondon`, `londoncafe`,
  `londonbrunch`, `specialitycoffee`, `matcha`, `dogfriendlylondon`.
- **Every post names the neighbourhood at least once** — "Finsbury Park", "N4" or
  "Stroud Green Road". This is what makes a post work for local search as well as for followers.
- `alt_text` is required and is written for a blind reader: describe what is in the frame, not
  how it feels.

### Per platform
| Platform | Caption length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `instagram` | 80–150 words | The first line is the hook; it is all most people see |
| `gbp` | **under 1,500 characters, aim for 100–200 words** | Google Business Profile post. Front-load the offer and the place name. No hashtags — they do nothing here |
| `facebook` | 40–80 words | Cross-post only. Do not write Facebook-first material |

---

## 4. If you are unsure

Write the post with the uncertain part marked in square brackets, like
`[CONFIRM — is the Christmas menu still running?]`, and leave `status: draft`.

A marked question is useful. A confident invention is damage.
